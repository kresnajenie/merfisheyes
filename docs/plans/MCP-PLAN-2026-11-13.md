## The biggest finding: SpatialData-DB already built half of goal 1

Researching SpatialData-DB for tomorrow, I read Tim Treis's client repo (`theislab/spatialdata-db-client`; he wrote nearly all of it). It has much of what you want for "research the library":
- **A versioned metadata schema with ontologies:**
  - organism, tissue and disease each as an ontology ID;
  - tissue and disease hierarchies (tissue system and organ, disease class and group);
  - `timepoint_days` (age as a number), `genotype`, `treatment` and `condition`;
  - a technology vocabulary, plus quality tiers and a review status.
- **A gene index** (`gene_index.parquet`): one row per dataset × gene, with total counts and the fraction of cells where it's detected. That's exactly what "datasets that have gene X" needs, without opening any files.
- **Cohort "primers"**: shared embeddings, UMAPs and QC across many datasets, e.g. a `mouse-brain-imaging` cohort.
- **Their own MCP server** (Python). It can query by facets and ranges, find datasets expressing genes, describe a dataset, produce citations, and download a cohort.

Our Explore import uses only a fraction of their columns, and keeps the rest in an unstructured JSON field.

**What this means:** we shouldn't invent a metadata standard. Adopt theirs for *every* MERFISHEYES dataset (BIL, community, yours), and produce the same catalog and gene-index files for our own data. Then your Alzheimer's question becomes **one server-side query** over both libraries:
- organism = mouse;
- region = brain, *including* sub-regions;
- age 30–365 days;
- expresses gene X above a threshold.

This is recorded as **D20, proposed**, pending your conversation with Tim. Their MCP and ours fit together: theirs fetches a cohort into Python, ours finds things across both libraries and shows them.

## How the collaborator's request would run with pillar D
*"Define radial glia vs immature neurons from pseudotime, UMAP and a molecule gradient; UMAP, spatial cells and molecules, dot plots, pseudotime; remove the bad FOV; gradient genes; into a .pptx"*:
1. The agent does the statistics in Python (pseudotime, UMAP, dot plots), as it does today.
2. It opens the data in local MERFISHEYES, pushes the radial glia / immature labels and pseudotime as columns, and hides the bad FOV.
3. It calls `meyes_snapshot`: cells coloured by label, cells by pseudotime, gradient genes as molecules. Each is a PNG with a legend, plus a link that reopens that exact view.
4. It builds the deck from the Python plots plus the MERFISHEYES spatial figures.

Today the agent would draw the spatial plots with matplotlib, losing MERFISHEYES's molecule rendering and 3D. That's the gap D closes.

## Ideas beyond the notes
- **Your data next to the library.** "Find the public dataset closest to my sample (same region, age, platform) and open both side by side." That combines goals 1 and 2 in one split screen, local on the left and public on the right. It could be the most compelling demo.
- **Every figure carries its view.** Each snapshot comes with a link that reopens that exact view, so figures are reproducible and the agent can write the "figure methods".
- **QC before analysis.** Many stores record QC numbers (counts per cell, an artifact-mask percentage). The agent can flag a bad FOV or section *before* the analysis, instead of the researcher spotting it afterwards.
- **SDDB's cohort embeddings, viewable.** Their shared UMAPs could open in MERFISHEYES.
- **"Live filtering"** could mean filtering inside a view (hide FOVs, expression thresholds) or filter controls on Explore. The plan covers both (D3 and B1), but tell me which you meant.

## Questions for Tim Treis

**Metadata and schema**
1. Is schema 3 meant as a standard others can *produce*? Could MERFISHEYES publish its own catalog and gene index for the BIL MERSCOPE data in that schema, checked by the SDDB client's validator? What compatibility promises come with schema versions?
2. Where should dataset-level metadata live: in the store itself (the root attributes, `sdata.attrs`), in the table's `uns`, or only in the catalog? Is a scverse convention coming?
3. How are `timepoint_days`, `development_stage` and `genotype` filled: by hand, from GEO, by an LLM? How does review work (`review_status`, publication verification)?
4. Mouse brain regions: UBERON, or the Allen mouse brain atlas ontology? Can we reuse their tissue hierarchy tables?
5. Mouse disease models (5xFAD, Trem2-R47H): MONDO plus a genotype field? Where do strain and sex go?
6. **Combined stores:** our BIL atlases hold 28–59 sections in one store, while every SDDB store has exactly one sample. Should a combined store be one catalog row, or one row per section pointing at the same store?

**The gene index**

7. How are `total_counts` and `fraction_obs_detected` computed: raw counts, from which table? How are control probes and blanks treated? Symbols or Ensembl IDs, and anything for mouse↔human orthologs?
8. Any plan for per-cell-type summaries (mean and fraction per cell type)? That's what "compare gene X across samples" really needs.

**Working together**

9. Could their catalog carry a `merfisheyes_url` column, as it does `vitessce_url`?
10. **CORS:** their bucket currently allows only www.merfisheyes.com. Could they add dev.merfisheyes.com and localhost / 127.0.0.1? Staging and the local viewer both need it.
11. Their MCP's roadmap. Two servers in one agent: shared IDs and conventions?
12. Is there a path for MERFISHEYES/BIL datasets to *join* SpatialData-DB (tiers, curation)?
13. Molecules at billion scale: is there interest in our multiscale points format upstream? `spatialdata` can't read it yet.
14. Are the cohort primers stable artifacts that others can build on?
